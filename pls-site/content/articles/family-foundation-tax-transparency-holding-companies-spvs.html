Start with the whole ownership structure

A family foundation may sit above several companies holding investments or property. Reviewing the foundation alone can therefore leave an important question unanswered: how is each company beneath it treated for UAE Corporate Tax? The FTA’s June 2026 Family Foundations Guide addresses these multi-tier structures. Its practical message is to assess the structure entity by entity, rather than assume that one approval covers every company.

Transparency is conditional

Article 17 of the Corporate Tax Law allows an eligible family foundation to apply for treatment as an Unincorporated Partnership. The conditions concern its beneficiaries, investment or savings purpose, activities and absence of a principal tax-avoidance purpose. It must not conduct an activity that would be a taxable business activity if undertaken directly by the relevant individuals. Additional prescribed conditions also matter.

This is a tax treatment requiring analysis; a DIFC or ADGM foundation registration does not by itself establish eligibility. Tax transparency generally attributes the relevant income, expenditure, assets and liabilities to the beneficiaries. Their own circumstances and the character of that income still need review.

Check every company in the chain

Ministerial Decision No. 261 of 2024 permits a juridical person wholly owned and controlled by a transparent family foundation to apply for transparent treatment, subject to its own conditions. Indirect ownership must run through an uninterrupted chain of entities themselves treated as Unincorporated Partnerships.

The June 2026 guide explains the consequence of an interruption: a non-transparent entity in the chain prevents companies beneath that entity from qualifying through this route. Each entity’s approval and continuing eligibility must therefore be checked. A wholly owned LLC is not automatically transparent simply because its ultimate owner is a family foundation.

A practical review example

Consider a hypothetical foundation owning Holding Company A, which owns Property SPV B. The review file should identify the legal owner and controller of each entity, its actual activities, the relevant tax periods and the status of each application. If A has not obtained the necessary transparent treatment, an approval for the foundation should not be treated as evidence that B is also transparent.

Before changing this structure, prepare a simple table showing each proposed transaction and the entity affected. Adding an outside investor, starting a trading activity or selling an intermediate holding company may change the facts on which an earlier assessment was based. Those changes deserve review before implementation, with the conclusion recorded in writing.

Build a file that can be maintained

A useful working file connects the legal documents to the accounting records. Avoid leaving the ownership chart, foundation charter and tax applications in separate folders with no explanation of how they fit together. Assign someone to keep the file current and record the reason for each change.

  • Keep dated ownership charts, incorporation documents and evidence of control for every tier.
  • Describe the assets and actual activities of each entity, using contracts and accounting records.
  • Retain application submissions, FTA approvals and the effective periods stated in them.
  • Record beneficiaries and how their relevant entitlements are identified.
  • Review proposed ownership and activity changes before completing them.

Approval is followed by annual compliance

FTA Decision No. 5 of 2025 requires an approved family foundation or qualifying owned juridical person to submit an annual confirmation within nine months after the relevant tax period. The confirmation addresses whether the conditions continued to be met. The decision also sets application timing and effective-period rules; the application date and approval should be reviewed together.
