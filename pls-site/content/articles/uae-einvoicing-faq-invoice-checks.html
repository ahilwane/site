Can we send an e-invoice to an overseas customer? What if the VAT rate is 0%? And what happens when the customer has not joined the network?

These are practical questions to settle with your accounting team and chosen Accredited Service Provider (ASP). Here are short answers and checks you can use when preparing your system.

1. How do we e-invoice an export?

If the overseas business is on Peppol, use its electronic address. If it is not, your ASP still reports the invoice data to the FTA, and you deliver the invoice to the customer separately, for example by email.

For an export-classified transaction with a receiver not registered in Peppol, the prescribed address is 0235:9900000099. An overseas address alone does not establish that the transaction qualifies as an export.

Check now: add the customer's country, available identifiers and network status to its customer record. Test how the customer will receive the invoice.

2. How do we e-invoice a supply charged at 0% VAT?

Use the zero-rated category Z, with a zero VAT rate and amount. Do not use invoice type 480 for a zero-rated supply: that type is for transactions exclusively outside the scope of VAT.

Example: if a reviewed supply of AED 12,000 qualifies for 0% VAT, the invoice shows AED 12,000 before VAT and AED 0 VAT. Zero-rated, exempt and outside-scope supplies need different tax mappings.

Check now: ask your software team what the system's “0%” code actually sends to your ASP.

3. How do we e-invoice a customer who has not joined the network yet?

During the rollout, a buyer may not yet be required to implement and may have no network identifier. The prescribed transitional route uses 0235:9900000098, with a regular tax invoice, such as a PDF, also delivered to the customer.

This is not a general solution for every missing identifier. Ask your ASP to confirm the buyer's position, particularly if its implementation deadline has already passed.

4. Is our TIN the same as our VAT TRN?

A TIN has 10 digits. A VAT TRN has 15 digits. They serve different purposes in e-invoicing. Do not enter a Corporate Tax TRN in the seller's VAT-identifier field.

Check now: confirm the numbers against your EmaraTax records and provider onboarding details. Avoid copying a number into every tax field simply because it belongs to the same company.

5. How do we invoice advance payments?

Invoice the advance when received. The later invoice covers the outstanding balance and refers to the advance invoice.

Example, before VAT: a contract is AED 24,000 and an advance of AED 6,000 has already been invoiced. The remaining invoice is AED 18,000. Link both records in your accounting system so the advance is not billed twice.

6. What changes when our customer is in a Free Zone?

Identify who ultimately benefits from the supply. If that is the customer, the beneficiary details can match. Otherwise, record the actual beneficiary.

Example: ask whether the customer is buying the service for its own staff or for another group company. Record the answer before preparing the invoice.

7. Can our provider store invoices for us?

Yes, storage can be arranged with the provider. Your business remains responsible for keeping the required records accessible.

Check now: ask how to retrieve an older invoice, export the records and keep access if you change providers. Test retrieval before relying on the arrangement.

8. Do we need HSN product codes now?

HSN codes are currently optional. Mandatory timing will be announced separately.

Check now: tidy your item descriptions and discuss product-data requirements with your provider so later changes are easier to manage.

9. Is emailing a PDF enough?

A PDF alone is not a structured e-invoice. It can serve as a customer-readable copy where appropriate, but it does not replace the required electronic data process.

Start with three invoices

Pick an export, a zero-rated sale and a customer not yet on the network. Ask your team to show the customer details, tax mapping, reporting result and delivery method for each. Record any missing information and give someone responsibility for resolving it.
