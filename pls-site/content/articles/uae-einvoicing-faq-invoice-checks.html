Before going live with UAE e-invoicing, test the invoices that need the most care: an export, a supply subject to 0% VAT and a sale to a customer who has not joined the network yet. Each test should connect the commercial records, the approved tax treatment and the invoice data produced by your accounting system.

For the wider readiness process, start with UAE E-Invoicing: Are You Ready? This companion FAQ focuses on the working file and software checks behind three everyday questions.

1. How do we prepare e-invoices for exports?

The FTA distinguishes delivery to a foreign buyer from reporting to the authority. Where an overseas buyer is not on Peppol, the supplier's provider still reports the data, and the supplier delivers the invoice to the buyer separately. [1]

Prepare a transaction file containing the contract or purchase order, customer details, invoice and relevant commercial support. Compare the legal name, country, address and available identifiers across those records. Document the reviewed treatment before the software team configures the invoice.

Ask your chosen Accredited Service Provider to demonstrate the relevant workflow using a sample from your business. Retain the reporting confirmation and the evidence of customer delivery together.

2. What should we check for e-invoices charged at 0% VAT?

PINT-AE identifies zero-rated supplies with category Z; exempt and outside-scope supplies have separate categories. [2] Your accounting-system label should represent the approved treatment accurately.

Review the tax codes staff currently select. A code named “0%” may be used inconsistently or connected to an unsuitable setting. Choose a representative invoice, document the approved treatment and compare the resulting invoice data with that decision.

Keep the test result and record who reviewed it. If your business has invoices with several types of supply, include one in the test set so the team can inspect the individual lines.

3. How do we e-invoice a customer who has not joined the network yet?

Confirm the buyer's onboarding position with your provider. The Ministry's transitional treatment is not a universal solution for missing identifiers. [3] Keep a dated record of the customer's response.

Give the relevant facts to your chosen provider and agree how the transaction will be processed and how the customer will receive its invoice. Assign someone to follow up when the customer's status changes, and include this scenario in testing before the first live invoice.

Keep a simple test record

  • Scenario and records: what is being supplied, to whom, and which supporting documents were reviewed?
  • Decision and owner: who confirmed the treatment and who maintains the customer and software settings?
  • Result and action: what did the test produce, what remains unresolved, and who will follow up?
