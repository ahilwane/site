UAE businesses need to prepare for e-invoicing before their mandatory implementation date. Readiness means checking your transactions, accounting records, software and service-provider arrangements so invoices can move through the required electronic process.

For businesses within scope with revenue of AED 50 million or more, the deadline to appoint an Accredited Service Provider is 30 October 2026, ahead of mandatory implementation on 1 January 2027. Smaller businesses have a later phase, giving them time to prepare their records and systems. [1]

What is UAE e-invoicing?

An e-invoice contains structured data that systems can process electronically. Under the UAE model, invoice information is exchanged through Accredited Service Providers, with tax data reported to the Federal Tax Authority.

A PDF, Word file, scan or invoice sent by email alone does not meet that definition. Businesses therefore need to review the flow of invoice data, rather than simply change the appearance of an invoice template. [2]

Which businesses are affected?

The framework covers business transactions within its scope, subject to specified exclusions. VAT registration is not the sole test: a business should assess its obligations even if it is not registered for VAT. [3]

Business-to-consumer transactions, and businesses conducting only those transactions, are currently excluded until a further ministerial decision determines otherwise. A business serving both companies and consumers should assess the relevant transactions separately. Other specific exclusions also apply. [4]

Recent changes businesses should know

Ministerial Decision No. 197 of 2026 introduced changes effective from 1 October 2026. Three points are particularly relevant to preparation: [5]

  • Transactions within a VAT group: transactions between members of the same registered VAT tax group are excluded for 24 months starting on 1 January 2027. Common ownership alone does not establish this exclusion, and transactions with parties outside the group require their own assessment.
  • Records and storage: the requirement to store e-invoice data within the UAE has been removed. Businesses must still retain the records for the statutory period and be able to provide them to the FTA.
  • Invoice timing: the amended general rule uses 14 business days from the date of the business transaction. This remains subject to the VAT-law timelines for VAT registrants; it should not be treated as a blanket extension of their invoicing deadlines.

A practical readiness checklist

  1. Confirm your scope and phase. Identify the business activities and transaction types involved, the relevant revenue figure, any exclusions and the dates that apply.
  2. Review customer and supplier data. Check legal names, addresses, identifiers, VAT registration details where applicable, currencies and invoice references. Resolve missing or inconsistent information before implementation.
  3. Check the accounting workflow. Trace a sale from order or contract through invoicing, customer receipt and ledger posting. Include incoming supplier invoices, advance payments, cancellations and credit notes in the review.
  4. Select and coordinate with an ASP. Use the Ministry of Finance's published provider information and confirm the provider's current status. Discuss compatibility with your accounting software, costs, onboarding, support and record retrieval.
  5. Test and assign responsibilities. Test outgoing and incoming invoices, corrections and rejected documents. Agree who reviews VAT treatment, monitors processing and follows up on errors. Keep a written plan with owners and completion dates.

These checks help turn a regulatory deadline into a manageable implementation project. A successful technical transmission should not be assumed to confirm that every accounting entry or VAT treatment is correct.
