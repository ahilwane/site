A nil Corporate Tax payment can still require a properly prepared return. For a company subject to UAE Corporate Tax, the accounting file should explain why the reported result is correct, even when there are no sales or no tax to pay.

1. Confirm the company and reporting period

Keep the licence, Corporate Tax registration details, financial year-end, relevant Tax Period and filing deadline together. Check the company's actual filing and deregistration position; an inactive year does not automatically mean its obligations have ended. 2

Use the period that applies to your company. Under the general nine-month rule, a normal Tax Period ending 31 December 2026 has a filing deadline of 30 September 2027. 4

2. Reconcile the bank and funding records

Reconcile every business bank account to the ledger. Identify outstanding items, bank charges and transfers between accounts. If shareholders paid costs personally or funded the business, record the nature of those transactions and retain the supporting documents.

No sales does not necessarily mean no transactions. Licence renewals, professional fees, shareholder loans and opening balances can still need to be recorded and explained.

3. Retain a complete revenue and expense file

Keep sales and purchase invoices, contracts, credit notes and evidence supporting the transactions. Reconcile receivables and payables and prepare an asset register showing purchases and disposals.

Where there is an accounting loss, prepare a separate reconciliation of the tax adjustments. An accounting loss is not automatically the final taxable result or a filing exemption. 1,3

4. Document any Small Business Relief election

If the business intends to elect for Small Business Relief, keep a revenue schedule for the relevant and all previous Tax Periods, together with an eligibility review and the election record. Eligible businesses still file a simplified return. 4,5

The current relief window includes qualifying Tax Periods ending on or before 31 December 2029 under Ministerial Decision No. 131 of 2026. Check the current conditions and exclusions before applying it. 6

5. Keep the final submission file

Retain the approved accounts, return workings, supporting schedules, relief elections, submitted return and submission acknowledgement. Keep any tax-payment confirmation where payment is due. Corporate Tax records generally need to be kept for at least seven years following the end of the relevant Tax Period. 7

A file you can hand over with confidence

The objective is an accounting file that another person can follow: which period it covers, how balances were reconciled, what adjustments were considered and why a relief was claimed. Start before the deadline so missing records can be identified and resolved.
