A customer pays by card, but the amount reaching your bank is smaller. Before treating the difference as missing income, build a bridge between the customer payment, the payment processor's settlement report and the bank receipt. Fees, refunds and settlement timing may explain it; an unexplained difference still needs investigation.

This guide is a practical bookkeeping workflow for UAE businesses. It does not determine the VAT treatment of a particular sale, fee or refund.

Why the bank receipt is only part of the picture

A processor may combine many customer payments into one payout and deduct charges before transferring the balance. The settlement date may also differ from the customer payment date. Recording only the bank deposit can leave customer invoices appearing unpaid or make sales and charges difficult to review.

Keep three records together: the customer invoices or sales report, the processor's transaction and settlement reports, and the bank statement. Each answers a different question: what was sold, how the payment was processed and what cash arrived.

1. Match the customer payments first

Use the transaction reference, invoice reference, date and amount to connect each card payment to the relevant sale. Check unsuccessful payments and cancellations separately. A payment attempt is not evidence that funds were collected.

If an invoice is already recorded, do not create another sale simply because the payment appears in a bank import. Check the existing entry and supporting evidence before changing or deleting anything.

2. Reconcile the processor settlement

For each payout, identify the customer collections included, refunds, processor charges and any other documented adjustments. A payment-clearing account can help track amounts collected but not yet settled. Agree the account mapping with the person responsible for the books.

Illustrative example: a settlement report shows AED 12,000 of customer collections, AED 500 of refunds and AED 240 of processor charges. With no other adjustments, the expected bank payout is AED 11,260. These are fictional gross cash amounts; the example does not calculate VAT or establish when revenue should be recognised.

If the bank receives AED 11,200 instead, the AED 60 difference should remain on an investigation list until supported. Do not force the reconciliation by posting an unexplained balancing expense.

3. Review timing and outstanding balances

A collection on the last day of the month may be paid out in the next month. List outstanding settlements by reference and age, then match them to later bank receipts. Separate an ordinary timing difference from a failed payout, a dispute or a chargeback.

Keep the original sale, refund evidence and any chargeback correspondence connected. A processor deduction alone does not establish that a sale was cancelled or that a tax adjustment is appropriate.

4. Use Zoho Books with a clear review trail

Before configuring imports or matching rules, agree how invoices, receipts, fees, refunds and transfers will be recorded. Test a small sample against the underlying reports. Use consistent references and attach supporting documents where the available workflow permits.

Review suggested matches before accepting them. Automation is useful when the mapping is correct; it does not replace checking an unusual amount or an unmatched settlement. Keep an export of the reconciled schedule with the monthly working papers.

A simple monthly control

  • Reconcile customer collections to processor reports, then settlements to the bank.
  • Give every unresolved item an owner, an explanation and a follow-up date.
  • Have a reviewer check old outstanding balances and approve supported corrections.

These steps support clearer accounts and make records easier to review for VAT and Corporate Tax preparation. They do not replace reviewing the tax treatment of the underlying transactions.

Official guidance and scope
